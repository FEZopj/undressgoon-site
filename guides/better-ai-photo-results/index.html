The five parts of a strong source image

You do not need studio photography. You do need enough clean visual information for the model to interpret the person and scene.

1. Framing

Keep the subject large in the frame without cutting through areas you expect the output to include. Avoid tiny full-body figures inside a wide room shot.

2. Pose

Front-facing and simple three-quarter poses are more predictable. Crossed limbs and extreme angles create ambiguous anatomy.

3. Lighting

Use even light with visible skin and clothing edges. Deep shadows and colored light can change shape and skin tone.

4. Sharpness

Use the original photo when possible. Motion blur, beauty filters and repeated compression remove details that the model needs.

5. Obstruction

Hands, long hair, bags, furniture and another person can interrupt body contours and confuse the edit boundary.

File choice

JPG, PNG and WebP are supported up to 12 MB. A clean source matters more than exporting a blurry image at a larger size.

Diagnose a weak result

Face or identity changed too much

Use a larger, sharper source with a visible face and less aggressive filtering. Extreme lighting can also make identity preservation harder.

Hands or limbs look distorted

Choose a frame where limbs are separated and hands are not crossing the body. Crop out unnecessary foreground objects.

The new outfit does not follow the body

Use clothing with visible contours and select a preset that makes sense for the existing pose. Very loose coats or blankets hide important structure.

The image looks soft or artificial

A low-resolution screenshot cannot be repaired by enlarging it. Return to the original camera image and avoid repeated social-media downloads.

Do not keep retrying a bad input

If the same structural problem appears twice, change the source photo or preset before using another credit. Repeating identical inputs is unlikely to solve unclear pose or obstruction.

Photo results FAQ

Why are hands or limbs distorted?

Overlapping limbs, hidden hands, motion blur and unusual angles can create ambiguous shapes. A clearer pose gives the model more reliable structure.

What image resolution works best?

Use a sharp original rather than a small screenshot or compressed copy. JPG, PNG and WebP files up to 12 MB are supported.

Should the subject face the camera?

A front-facing or simple three-quarter pose usually produces the most predictable result, especially for the first test.

Can I upload more than one person?

The generator is designed around one clear adult subject. Multiple people can create identity and anatomy conflicts.
